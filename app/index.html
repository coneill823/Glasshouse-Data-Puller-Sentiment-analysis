    What these numbers are, and what they are not

    Every figure here is arithmetic over recorded acts. There are no word lists, no opinion about which side of a vote is the good one, and no score for whether someone is “pro” or “anti” anything. Turnout is divisions they voted in. Broke with party counts the divisions where they voted against their own party's majority — each one listed, so you can check it. Works on counts which subjects their questions and speeches mention.

    The most important thing the data shows is how little room there is between members: the typical representative votes with their party on 99–100% of divisions in all four legislatures. Party agreement is shown for completeness, but it separates almost nobody — voting is whipped, and that is the finding, not a flaw in the measurement. What people choose to spend their time on varies far more than how they vote.

    Two honest limits. Turnout covers a member's current spell in the chamber only. Plenty of them served, lost their seat and returned years later; counting the whole span would charge them for divisions held when they were not members at all. Genuine absence still shows — illness, leave, a suspension — because the count runs to the most recent division, not to their own last vote. And sorting a speech into a subject uses keyword lists we wrote: that is categorisation rather than political judgement, but it is the one place a human list still touches these numbers.
