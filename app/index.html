    How these grades are made

    Every question, speech and division vote from each representative is scored against word lists for 20 policy topics. A topic only scores when the statement is actually about it, and votes count double, because how someone votes is unambiguous where wording is not.

    A score runs from −1 to +1 and is always read as pro that topic: pro   against. Scores are shrunk toward zero when there is little evidence, so a single remark cannot look like a conviction. A few topics need their axis in mind: Crime & justice means pro-law-and-order, Immigration & asylum means pro-welcoming, and Economy & finance means pro-public-spending.

    This is transparent word-list scoring, not a judgement of a person. It cannot read sarcasm, and it can misread a topic attacked in wording it otherwise supports — treat it as a starting point and check the record itself.
