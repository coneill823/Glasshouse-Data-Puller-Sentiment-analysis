# Glasshouse privacy policy

> **Draft.** This describes what the website and the apps do, as built. Before
> publishing it, replace the two placeholders in *Who we are*, check the links,

Last updated: 22 November 2026

## The short version

Glasshouse has no accounts, no adverts and no analytics, and we run no server
that receives anything about you. Your quiz answers never leave your device.

## Who we are

Glasshouse is independent. It is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any
parliament, government, political party or the organisations whose open data
it uses.

## What stays on your device

- **Your quiz answers**, which statements you marked as mattering a lot, and
  how far through each part of the quiz you are.
- **The place you last looked up** — a postcode or a constituency, and for a
  postcode the name of its ward and the official codes of the areas it is
  in — so that your representatives, councillors and upcoming elections are
  there when you come back.

In the apps this is kept in the app's own private storage; on the website, in
your browser's local storage. It is not sent to us or to anyone else, and it is
not backed up to us. Your answers say something about your political opinions,
which is why they are handled this way.

To remove it: choose **Clear my answers and start again**, or clear the app's
data, or uninstall the app. On the website, clear the site's data in your
browser.

## What leaves your device

- **A postcode, if you search by one.** It is sent over an encrypted
  connection to [postcodes.io](https://postcodes.io), an open postcode lookup
  service, which replies with the constituencies, ward and council that cover
  it. We never receive it. Your councillors and the elections coming up are
  then found on your own device from that reply; the postcode is not sent to
  Democracy Club, to any council or to anyone else. Searching by the name of
  a constituency or a person sends nothing. postcodes.io's own policy
  describes what it keeps.
- **Requests for newer voting data (apps only).** At most twice a day the app
  downloads the latest public data files from this project's repository on
  [GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
  As with any download, GitHub sees the internet address the request came
  from. The request contains nothing about you or your answers.
- **Links you choose to open.** Links to the votes behind each figure, to an
  election or to a councillor's page open TheyWorkForYou, a parliament's or
  council's own website, or Democracy Club's WhoCanIVoteFor in your browser.
  Those sites have their own policies.

If you use the website rather than an app, whoever hosts it will see the
ordinary record every web server keeps of the pages it sends, and the page
loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, which sees the internet address that
request came from. Neither request carries your answers or your postcode.

## What we collect

Nothing. There is nothing to sign in to, and the apps contain no advertising
or tracking software.

## People who appear in Glasshouse

Glasshouse shows information about people in public office and people
standing for it. All of it was already published, by a parliament, a council
or an election register, and it is shown because of the public role:

- **Members and former members of the four parliaments, and peers**: their
  name, party, seat and votes, from TheyWorkForYou (mySociety) and the
  legislatures' own records.
- **Candidates in an upcoming election**: their name, the party they are
  standing for and where, from [Democracy Club](https://democracyclub.org.uk).
  Nothing else that register holds is copied, and candidates are not scored.
- **Councillors, for the councils read so far**: their name, party, ward and,
  where the council publishes it, how many meetings they attended.

If something shown about you is wrong, write to the address above. Where the
error is in the source, the body that published it can correct it there, and
the next build of Glasshouse will follow.

## Children

Glasshouse is written for adults of voting age and is not directed at children.
